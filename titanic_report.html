What was done:
  • Missing Age (177 rows) — imputed using the median age within each Passenger Class + Sex group (more accurate than a single global median).
  • Missing Embarked (2 rows) — filled with the most frequent port (mode).
  • Missing Cabin (687 rows, 77%) — too sparse to impute reliably, so instead of dropping the column entirely, converted it into a binary HasCabin feature (having a recorded cabin correlates with class/survival).
  • Outliers in Fare — detected via the IQR method and capped (Winsorized) rather than deleted, preserving all 891 records while reducing distortion from extreme values (max fare was 512 vs. a median of ~14).
  • Duplicates — checked on full rows and PassengerId; none found in this dataset.
  • Feature engineering — extracted Title from passenger names, and derived FamilySize / IsAlone from SibSp + Parch.

4. Key Findings: Who Survived?

Storytelling — the data says:
  • Overall survival rate was 38.4% — but this hides huge disparities.
  • "Women and children first" held true: women survived at far higher rates than men in every class, and 1st-class women survived at the highest rate of any group.
  • Class mattered enormously: 1st class passengers had roughly 2-3x the survival rate of 3rd class passengers, likely reflecting cabin location and lifeboat access.
  • Small families fared better than solo travelers or very large families — a family size of 2-4 had the best survival odds, while being alone or in a family of 6+ hurt your chances.
  • Fare correlates with survival, largely because fare is a proxy for class and cabin location.
